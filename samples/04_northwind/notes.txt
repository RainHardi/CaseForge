Dispatch Optimizer

Forwarded message from Priya Raman, project manager, subject line "Meridian notes before I
forget": The internal name was Meridian but Calloway Freight Group calls it the Dispatch
Optimizer, so both names appear in documents. Calloway is headquartered in Rotterdam and
operates 14 warehouses across the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany. Contract signed 9
February 2024, kickoff on 4 March 2024. Original go-live was 30 September 2024 but the system
went live on 18 November 2024 because the client's SAP S/4HANA migration delayed our API
access by roughly six weeks. Contract value was 412,000 euros and the final invoiced amount
ended at 438,500 euros after two change requests. CR-03 added offline mode to the driver mobile
app, CR-07 added carbon emissions reporting per route.

Team headcount began at 6 people and reached 11 in August 2024, then dropped to 7 for the
stabilization phase. I think the final count on the payroll sheet was 9 distinct people over the whole
project because two contractors left before October. Company is Northwind Digital Works, about
85 employees, offices in Utrecht and Lisbon. The Lisbon office handled the mobile app and half of
QA.

Typed from Tomas Ferreira's notebook (backend lead): the routing engine is written in Python 3.11
with Google OR-Tools for the vehicle routing problem solver. First prototype used a simple
nearest-neighbour heuristic and produced routes about 4 percent shorter than the client's manual
planning. After we added time-window constraints, driver break rules from EU Regulation
561/2006 and vehicle capacity limits, the average improvement rose to 11.8 percent in the pilot on
the Antwerp and Ghent warehouses. Solver time for 220 stops was 94 seconds in the first build,
reduced to 9 seconds after moving the distance matrix calculation to a precomputed cache in
Redis. Database is PostgreSQL 15 with the PostGIS extension. Message queue is RabbitMQ.
Deployed on AWS eu-west-1 using Terraform and ECS Fargate. Around 60 percent of the
infrastructure code was reused from the earlier Harbor Tracker project.

Design notes from Amira Haddad (UX designer), written during week 3: interviewed 9 dispatchers
and 12 drivers at the Rotterdam and Duisburg sites. Main complaint from dispatchers was that the
previous tool needed 15 to 20 minutes every morning to build routes in a spreadsheet. Drivers said
the paper manifests were frequently outdated by the time they left the yard. Created 3 rounds of
Figma prototypes, tested with 6 users in the second round, and the task completion rate for editing
a route moved from 58 percent to 93 percent between round 1 and round 3. Chose a high-contrast
palette because drivers use the app in truck cabs with strong sunlight. Button minimum size 48
pixels. The dispatcher dashboard uses a map-first layout with a collapsible list on the left side.

Metrics sent by Priya, Friday 22 November 2024, three days after go-live: 1,340 routes planned in
the first 72 hours, 96.2 percent of drivers logged in successfully on the first day, planning time per
morning reduced from about 18 minutes to 3 minutes for each dispatcher. By February 2025 the
client reported fuel consumption down 9.4 percent across pilot warehouses and late deliveries
reduced from 14 percent to 6.1 percent. Annual savings estimate given by the Calloway finance
director was 1.1 million euros, although our team has not verified that number independently.

Mobile app, from Lucas Oliveira (Lisbon): React Native 0.73, TypeScript, SQLite for local storage
and a custom sync layer so drivers can keep working without a connection for up to 8 hours.
Supports Dutch, German, French and Polish because many drivers are Polish speakers. Barcode
scanning for proof of delivery with the device camera, signature capture, and photo upload with
automatic compression below 400 KB. The offline mode was the hardest part: we had 3 conflict
resolution bugs found in October and fixed by 29 October. App store release for Android only
because Calloway uses Zebra Android handhelds. About 410 active devices in January 2025.

Problems and lessons collected during the retrospective meeting on 12 December 2024 (notes by
Sofia Marin, QA lead): API documentation from the client was incomplete, so we wrote a mock
server of the SAP interface to continue development, which saved roughly 5 weeks but caused 14
defects when the real API behaved differently. Automated test coverage ended at 82 percent for
the backend and 64 percent for the mobile app. Total of 611 test cases, 187 of them automated
end to end with Playwright. Load test with 1,500 simulated concurrent drivers passed with a 95th
percentile response time of 420 milliseconds. Two production incidents in the first month: a
timezone error for the Poland border routes on 24 November and a cache invalidation problem on
2 December. Both resolved in under 4 hours.

Short message from Daniel Okafor (DevOps) in the team channel: pipeline uses GitHub Actions,
average build time 7 minutes, deployments went from monthly in the old Northwind process to 2 or
3 per week on this project. Monitoring with Grafana and Prometheus, alerts to PagerDuty. Added a
carbon emissions module in November using the GLEC Framework emission factors, which is
what the client asked for in CR-07.

Client feedback, quoted from the email of Henrik de Vries, operations director at Calloway, 20
January 2025: he said it was the first software delivery in five years that the dispatchers asked to
keep using instead of requesting changes to. He also mentioned the training sessions were useful.
We ran 6 training sessions of 90 minutes each, 74 dispatchers and team leaders trained in total. A
follow-up contract for phase 2 (extending to 9 additional warehouses in France and Spain) was
signed on 3 March 2025 with an estimated value near 290,000 euros.
